POWER GAIN FOR THE TWO LAGARIAS MULTIPLIER COUNTS
6 November 2026. Complete candidate proof for independent review.
Attribution for any eventual manuscript: Timofei Iuzhakov only.
Internal research note: no publication or priority claim is made here.

0. STATEMENT

Let D be the nonnegative integers with ordinary ternary digits in {0,1}.
For real X>=1 define
 MC(X)=#{1<=M<=X: some integer N>0 has N,MN in D},
 MH(X)=#{1<=M<=X: dim_H{x in Z3:x,Mx in Sigma}>0},
where Sigma is the 3-adic {0,1} Cantor set. Put
 s=log_3 2, phi=(1+sqrt(5))/2, g=log_3 phi,
 alpha=(s-g)/(1+g),
 beta=s+(2s-1)*alpha.
Then there are absolute constants c>0 and X0 such that

 MC(X) >= c X^beta,     MH(X) >= c X^beta       (X>=X0).       (T)

Numerically alpha=0.1341512347221138... and
 beta=0.6660585297784036... > s=0.6309297535714574... .

This is a bound for integer multipliers M, not the count of powers 2^n
whose digits omit 2. All analytic and combinatorial ingredients needed
below are proved explicitly. The proof does not invoke the causal
container theorem, finite-rank unit equations, or a conjectural mixing
statement. One input is the one-distance forcing mechanism already
present in the archived audit; its full finite-word proof is supplied.

1. FINITE CANTOR WORDS AND AN ELEMENTARY SAMPLING INEQUALITY

Let D_l={sum_(j=0)^(l-1) epsilon_j 3^j:epsilon_j in {0,1}}, and set
 F_l(t)=2^(-l) sum_(z in D_l) exp(2*pi*i*z*t), f_l(t)=|F_l(t)|.
Then
 F_l(t)=product_(j=0)^(l-1) (1+exp(2*pi*i*3^j*t))/2,
 0<=f_l<=1,
 f_(l+l')(t)=f_l(t)*f_l'(3^l t).                              (1)

Sampling lemma. If 0<delta<=1, T is a finite subset of R/Z with circular
separation at least delta, and P(t)=sum_(j=0)^K c_j exp(2*pi*i*j*t), then

 sum_(t in T)|P(t)|^2 <= (delta^(-1)+4*pi*K) sum_j |c_j|^2.     (2)

Proof. Center disjoint half-open arcs I_t of length delta at the points.
For G=|P|^2 and u in I_t, G(t)<=G(u)+integral_(I_t)|G'|.
Integrating in u and summing gives
 sum G(t)<=delta^(-1) integral G + integral |G'|.
The latter integral is at most 2||P||_2 ||P'||_2, which by Parseval is
at most 4*pi*K||P||_2^2. This proves (2). Endpoint overlaps have measure
zero and do not affect the argument.

The exact coefficient identities are
 integral_0^1 |F_h|^2 = 2^(-h),
 integral_0^1 |F_h|^4 = (3/8)^h.                            (3)
For the second, the coefficients of F_h^2 count sums of two D_h words.
In each ternary position the possible sums 0,1,2 have multiplicities
1,2,1; there is no carry. The sum of squared unnormalized coefficients
is therefore (1^2+2^2+1^2)^h=6^h, and normalization is 16^h.
The degrees of F_h and F_h^2 are (3^h-1)/2 and 3^h-1 respectively.

2. A FAREY FOURIER BOUND AT POLYNOMIAL LENGTH

For real D>=2 let
 T_D={v/d modulo1:2<=d<=D, gcd(d,3)=1, 1<=v<d, gcd(v,d)=1}.
Its distinct points have circular separation at least D^(-2).
Put h=ceil(2 log_3 D). Thus D^2<=3^h<3D^2.
Applying (2)-(3), with a universal constant C, gives
 sum_(t in T_D) f_h(t)^2 <= C(3/2)^h,
 sum_(t in T_D) f_h(t)^4 <= C(9/8)^h.
By Cauchy--Schwarz,
 sum_(t in T_D) f_h(t)^3 <= C(27/16)^(h/2) <= C' D^eta,
 eta=log_3(27/16)<s,                                      (4)
because 27/16<2.

If L>=3h, put p=floor(L/h)>=3. Discarding the remaining factors in (1)
and applying Holder with p blocks yields
 sum_(t in T_D) f_L(t)
 <= product_(j=0)^(p-1) (sum_(t in T_D) f_h(3^(jh)t)^p)^(1/p)
 = sum_(t in T_D) f_h(t)^p
 <= sum_(t in T_D) f_h(t)^3 <= C' D^eta.                    (5)
The equality holds because multiplication by 3^(jh) permutes T_D: each
denominator d is coprime to 3 and the numerator remains coprime to d.
No factor |T_D| is lost in this unnormalized Holder inequality.

For every fixed D, the left side in (5) tends to zero as L tends to
infinity. Indeed, each t=v/d in the finite set has a finite periodic
orbit under multiplication by 3, avoiding zero. Every factor is at most
1, and at least one (in fact every) orbit factor |cos(pi*3^j v/d)| is
strictly below 1, so the product tends to zero. This also covers d=2,
where a factor may equal zero exactly.

3. MOST CANTOR DENOMINATORS MIX AT THIS POLYNOMIAL LENGTH

Let Y>=3 and
 S_Y={N in D:1<=N<=Y, N=1 mod3}.
Elementary word counting gives
  (1/4)Y^s <= |S_Y| <= 2Y^s.                              (6)
For the lower bound take all words of length floor(log_3 Y) with low
digit 1. For the upper bound allow all words of length
K=floor(log_3 Y)+1.

For any integer 1<=d<=Y, let T_Y(d)=#{N in S_Y:d divides N}. Then

 T_Y(d) <= 4 (Y/d)^s.                                    (7)

To see this, put j=floor(log_3 d). Fixing the ternary digits at positions
j through K-1 covers D_K by 2^(K-j) intervals, each with length
(3^j-1)/2<d. Each interval contains at most one multiple of d.
Since 3^K<=3Y and 3^j>d/3, 2^(K-j)<4(Y/d)^s, proving the bound.

For N prime to 3 set
 R_N(L)=sum_(v=1)^(N-1) f_L(v/N), with R_1(L)=0.
Grouping each nonzero fraction v/N by its reduced denominator d gives
the exact identity
 sum_(N in S_Y) R_N(L)
 =sum_(2<=d<=Y,3 does not divide d) T_Y(d)
    sum_(1<=v<d,gcd(v,d)=1) f_L(v/d).                      (8)

Claim. As Y,L tend to infinity with
 L>=3 ceil(2 log_3 Y),
 one has
  sum_(N in S_Y) R_N(L) = o(Y^s).                         (9)

Proof. Write J=floor(log_2 Y), and use the bins (2^(j-1),2^j] for
1<=j<=J, followed, if nonempty, by (2^J,Y]. For the j-th complete bin,
(7) and (5) bound its contribution divided by Y^s by
 C 2^(-js) sum_(t in T_(2^j)) f_L(t) <= C' 2^(-j(s-eta)).
This is summable over j since eta<s. For each fixed j its actual
contribution divided by Y^s tends to zero, by the fixed-D observation
after (5). Given epsilon, first choose a fixed cutoff j0 making the
sum of the uniform bounds for j>j0 less than epsilon, then take L
large enough for the finitely many remaining terms. The final partial
bin is bounded by C Y^(eta-s), since 2^J>=Y/2, and also tends to zero.
This proves (9). All constants are uniform in the particular Y,L.

Consequently, for all sufficiently large Y,L in this range, the good set
 G={N in S_Y:R_N(L)<=1/2}
satisfies
 |G| >= (1/8)Y^s.                                       (10)
Indeed the number of excluded N is at most 2 sum_N R_N(L)=o(Y^s).

4. MANY MULTIPLIERS FROM EACH GOOD DENOMINATOR

Set L=floor(log_3 X). Suppose Y tends to infinity and
 L>=3 ceil(2 log_3 Y).
For N in G choose L_N=floor(log_3(NX)). Then L_N>=L and (1) gives
R_N(L_N)<=R_N(L)<=1/2. Character orthogonality gives
 #{z in D_(L_N):N divides z} >= (2^(L_N)/N)(1-R_N(L_N))
                              >=2^(L_N-1)/N.
Each such z satisfies z<=3^(L_N)<=NX and determines M=z/N<=X;
removing z=0 therefore yields, for
 A_N(X)={1<=M<=X:NM in D},
 |A_N(X)| >= (1/4)X^s N^(s-1)-1.                         (11)
As s<1, (10)-(11) give
 sum_(N in G)|A_N(X)|
 >=(1/32)X^s Y^(2s-1)-2Y^s
 >=(1/64)X^s Y^(2s-1)                                   (12)
provided X^s Y^(s-1) tends to infinity. This condition will hold for
our choice Y=c0 X^alpha below.

5. THE GOLDEN FINITE-WORD OVERLAP BOUND

Let r in 1+3Z3, r!=1, e=v3(r-1)>=1. For n>=0 define
 W_n(r)={x in D_n: rx mod3^n belongs to D_n}.
Let H_n(e) count binary words of length n with no two 1s distance e
apart. Then
 |W_n(r)| <= H_n(e) <= phi^(n+e).                         (13)
This holds for all locally admissible words, whether or not extendable.

Full forcing proof. Write r=1+3^e u, with u a 3-adic unit. If the input
digits are x_j, write u*x=sum y_j3^j, y_j in {0,1,2}. In the addition
x+3^e ux let gamma_q be the incoming carry at digit q. It is 0 or 1.
At q>=e put B_q=y_(q-e)+gamma_q in {0,1,2,3}. Among choices x_q in
{0,1}, admissibility of (B_q+x_q) mod3 gives the following exact table:
 B=0: both choices, next carry0;
 B=1: only x_q=0, next carry0;
 B=2: only x_q=1, next carry1;
 B=3: both choices, next carry1.
Thus, on admissible paths, gamma_(q+1)=1_(B_q>=2), independent of the
chosen admissible value of x_q. For q<e the carry is zero.

Fix a prefix x_<i. The preceding identity implies that gamma_(i+e)
is determined by y_0,...,y_(i-1), hence by x_<i, independently of all
admissible choices in positions i,...,i+e-1. Also
 y_i = u_0*x_i + R_i(x_<i) modulo3, where u_0=1 or2.
Therefore the output digit at i+e is
 x_(i+e)+u_0*x_i+Q_i(x_<i) modulo3.                       (14)
Among x_i=0,1 at least one makes its offset nonzero modulo3, which
allows at most one admissible choice of x_(i+e), regardless of the
intermediate admissible digits. Mark that future position as forced.

For completeness, the tree comparison does not assume a common forced
digit across branches. A mark means only that each later admissible
prefix on that branch has at most one child at the marked position.
Maintain the set S of such future marks. At a marked current position
there is at most one child. At an unmarked position with only one child
we may simply keep S. If two children exist, at least one has the
additional mark i+e, when i+e<n; the other retains S. Other actual
restrictions may be discarded in an upper bound. Backwards induction
compares the number of completions with the number of independent sets
of the distance-e graph on remaining positions, with positions in S
required absent. Indeed the graph's deletion recursion at an unmarked
i is precisely the sum of the counts without and with the additional
mark i+e, and at a marked i it has a single continuation. Deleting an
additional mark can only increase this comparison count. Thus the
initial empty-mark count is H_n(e), proving the first inequality.

The distance-e graph is the disjoint union of at most e paths, one for
each residue modulo e. A path of m vertices has F_(m+2) independent
sets, and F_(m+2)<=phi^(m+1), by the Fibonacci recurrence with base
cases 1<=phi and 2<=phi^2. Multiplying yields the second inequality
in (13), including n<e by using at most min(e,n) paths.

6. SUMMING THE ACTUAL 3-ADIC DISTANCES

For distinct N,N' in S_Y put e=v3(N'-N)>=1 and r=N'/N.
If M belongs to A_N intersect A_N', then x=NM<=XY and x,rx are
ordinary Cantor integers. The map M->x is injective. With
 n=ceil(log_3(XY+1)), (13) gives
 |A_N intersect A_N'| <=phi^(n+e)<=C (XY)^g phi^e.          (15)

The factor phi^e must be summed, not replaced by its worst-case value.
Fix N in S_Y and use K=floor(log_3 Y)+1. For every e>=1 with e<K,
 #{N' in S_Y:N'=N modulo3^e}<=2^(K-e)<=2Y^s 2^(-e).
No distinct N' has v3(N'-N)>=K. As |S_Y|<=2Y^s,
 sum_(unordered distinct N,N' in S_Y) phi^v3(N'-N)
 <=2Y^(2s) sum_(e>=1) (phi/2)^e <= C Y^(2s),              (16)
where using the count for v3>=e instead of v3=e only increases the
bound. The series converges because phi<2.
Combining (15)-(16), even after restricting to G, yields
 sum_(N<N' in G)|A_N intersect A_N'| <=C0 X^g Y^(2s+g).    (17)

7. PARAMETER CHOICE AND BONFERRONI

Take alpha=(s-g)/(1+g), Y=c0 X^alpha with a sufficiently small fixed
c0>0. We have 0<alpha<1/6. For an exact elementary check of the latter,
s<7/11 since 2^11<3^7, and g>3/7 since phi^7=13phi+8>27. The function
(s-g)/(1+g) increases in s and decreases in g, so alpha<8/55<1/6.
It follows that L=floor(log_3 X)>=3 ceil(2 log_3 Y) for large X.
Also X^sY^(s-1) tends to infinity, since alpha<1/6 and s>1/2.
Thus (12) and (17) apply.

Their ratio is at most
 64C0 X^(g-s)Y^(1+g)=64C0 c0^(1+g),
by the definition of alpha. Choose c0 so that this is at most1/2.
Bonferroni now gives
 MC(X)>=|union_(N in G) A_N(X)|
 >=(1/128) X^sY^(2s-1)
 = (c0^(2s-1)/128) X^(s+(2s-1)alpha).
This proves the claimed MC lower bound.

8. PASSAGE TO MH AND DEPENDENCIES

If N,MN are positive Cantor integers, choose B with 3^B>max(N,MN).
Then for every sequence epsilon_j in {0,1},
 x=N sum_(j>=0) epsilon_j 3^(Bj)
has only digits0/1, as does Mx: both consist of nonoverlapping copies
of the respective finite block. This embeds a scaled B-block Cantor
set, of dimension log2/(B log3)>0, into C(1,M). Consequently MH>=MC,
and (T) follows for MH as well.

Dependency graph:
 exact digital energy (3) + elementary sampling (2)
   -> Farey Fourier moment (4) + Holder permutation (5)
   -> Cantor divisor multiplicity (7) + dyadic summation
   -> most-denominator mixing (9) -> first moment (12);
 archived one-distance carry forcing, reproved in section5
   -> golden finite-prefix count (13)
   -> valuation-level summation (16) -> overlap bound (17);
 first moment + overlaps -> Bonferroni -> MC -> spaced-copy Cantor -> MH.

The general causal-container theorem is not an assumption. The relation
to that core is the shared causal carry property: future admissibility
depends on an already-read prefix, which is used here only in a
one-distance tree. The reverse automaton for 621*3^(4k)-20 is a separate
negative witness criterion; it is compatible with this abundance result
and supplies no bound used here. Neither argument settles the dimensions
of the finite-visit limit Gamma or the specific orbit starting at1.
