PARAMETRIC REVERSE CARRY INVARIANT — 6 November 2026
Candidate theorem for independent hostile review. Internal research proof, not externally refereed.

THEOREM. For every integer k>=0, M_k=621*3^(4k)-20 cannot equal b/a for positive integers a,b whose ordinary ternary digits all belong to {0,1}. Moreover M_k lies outside the elementary exceptional sets D,E in Bai--Meleshko--Riasat--Shallit, so M_k belongs to their sporadic-exception set F. In particular F is infinite.

Notation warning: the register label A below is a BINARY encoding of an m-digit queue. It is NOT its ordinary ternary value, and no identity c=23A-d for the ordinary multiplication carry is asserted. The proof uses the digit identity directly.

1. Exact delayed carry model
Put m=4k+3, so M_k=23*3^m-20. If M_k*a=b with ternary digits a_j,b_j in {0,1}, then
  b+20a=23*3^m*a.
Set x_j=a_(j-m), with a_h=0 for h<0. The integer carries satisfy
  d_0=0,
  d_(j+1)=(d_j+20a_j+b_j-23x_j)/3.                  (1)
These are integers: the partial sum of the digit identity below position j is divisible by 3^j. Equivalently, sum_(h<j)(20a_h+b_h-23a_(h-m))*3^h=3^j*d_j. For j beyond all digit supports and the delay, this partial sum is the full zero sum, so d_j=0.

Every carry lies in I={-11,-10,...,10}. Indeed, for d in I and binary a,b,x the numerator in (1) lies between -34 and 31. When it is divisible by 3 it lies between -33 and 30; induction gives the assertion.

Let A_j=sum_(i=0)^(m-1) a_(j-m+i)*2^i, the binary queue of the most recent m input digits, with the oldest digit in bit 0. Then x_j=A_j mod2 and
  A_(j+1)=floor(A_j/2)+a_j*2^(m-1).                (2)
The initial state is (A_0,d_0)=(0,0). Any finite witness gives a finite path back to (0,0), containing an input digit a_j=1. Since M_k is a 3-adic unit, a common power of 3 can first be divided out, so we may assume a_0=b_0=1. Its first step has d_1=7 and A_1=2^(m-1), hence leaves (0,0).

Consider a transition into (0,0). Equation (2) forces a_j=0 and A_j=x_j in {0,1}. Equation (1) forces d_j=23x_j-b_j. If x_j=1 this is 22 or23, outside I. If x_j=0, the predecessor is either (0,0) or (0,-1). The first return to (0,0) after the nontrivial initial step must therefore pass through (0,-1). To exclude a positive witness it suffices to exhibit a set S_m containing (0,-1), closed under ALL predecessors in the state space {0,...,2^m-1} x I, and omitting (0,0).

2. Exact inverse transition
For a current state (A,d), let a=floor(A/2^(m-1)) be its most significant bit. For each choice x,b in {0,1}, its potential predecessor is
  A^- = 2A-a*2^m+x,
  d^- = 3d-20a-b+23x,                              (3)
and it is a valid predecessor exactly when d^- belongs to I. The binary label A^- automatically belongs to {0,...,2^m-1}. Formula (3) enumerates ALL predecessors; b is the output digit of the original forward transition.

6. Membership in the sporadic set F
Use the definitions in Bai et al., section1.4:
 The original C consists of positive ordinary ternary0/2 integers. If C_01 denotes the positive ternary0/1 integers used here, then C=2*C_01 and C/C=C_01/C_01. Thus V_int=N intersect(C/C)=N intersect(C_01/C_01),
 D={N:N congruent2*3^(i-1) mod3^i for some i>=1},
 E=N intersect union_(i>=0)[(3/2)3^i,2*3^i], F=N\(V_int union D union E).
The theorem above gives M_k notin V_int. Since M_k congruent1 mod3, it does not lie in D. For m>=3,
  2*3^(m+2) < 23*3^m-20 < (3/2)*3^(m+3).
This is the gap between consecutive intervals defining E, so M_k notin E. Therefore M_k in F for all k>=0. The M_k are distinct and tend to infinity.

7. Relation to the primary literature and limits
Bai--Meleshko--Riasat--Shallit (2022), arXiv2202.13694, section1.4, ask whether F is infinite and suggest the family621*3^(4k)-20. The latest primary text located in this audit is Vamshi Jandhyala, 'Infinitely many integers that are not quotients of zero-free balanced ternary numbers', version3 dated24October2026, section6. It verifies k=4 and explicitly does not claim an all-k proof for this0/1 family. Its principal balanced +/-1 theorems concern a different digit set.
