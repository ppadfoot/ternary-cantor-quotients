3. SHARED DEFINITIONS AND THE TWO CONCLUSIONS

D contains ordinary nonnegative integers with finite ternary digits
0 and 1, and D^+ removes zero. The quotient set uses positive numerator
and denominator. Its positive integer members are exactly those counted
by M_C. M_H instead counts positive-dimensional 3-adic intersections.
The distinction is preserved. The original 0/2 integer-digit convention
has positive members 2 D^+, so it gives the same quotient set; zero
does not accidentally enter a denominator.

The first theorem gives an eventual lower bound c X^beta for both
counts, with beta=s+(2s-1)(s-g)/(1+g), s=log_3(2), g=log_3(phi),
beta approximately 0.6660585297784. It is not stated as a full asymptotic,
a density theorem, or a bound on exceptional dyadic exponents.

The second theorem concerns every k>=0:
621*3^(4k)-20 = 23*3^(4k+3)-20 is not a quotient of positive finite
0/1-digit integers. These integers also avoid the two named elementary
obstruction classes, so the claim is an infinite exceptional family in
the stated sense. It does not assert that the corresponding 3-adic
intersection is empty or has Hausdorff dimension zero.

4. POWER LOWER-BOUND PROOF

The elementary sampling estimate, exact second and fourth Fourier
moments, interpolation and Holder block-permutation step retain their
hypotheses, including denominators coprime to 3. Reduced-denominator
sampling uses Farey separation, and the divisor multiplicity over Cantor
denominators is controlled before summing dyadic ranges. The dominated
tail argument keeps Y and L coupled in the required range rather than
mistakenly using pointwise convergence uniformly. N=1 and the harmless
Y>=3 endpoint convention are retained.

The common product length is passed to each L_N using monotonicity of
Fourier moduli. Orthogonality, floor losses and removal of the zero
numerator give the claimed positive-witness first moment. The count of
good unit denominators and its Y^(2s-1) contribution are preserved.

The overlap estimate is for all locally valid finite words. The carry
analysis and marked-tree exclusion justify the golden-ratio path count;
it does not assume a common forced digit or infinite extendability.
The pair sum averages the actual valuation v_3(N-M), using the Cantor
prefix count, rather than replacing it by the largest possible distance.
This gives the stated X^g Y^(2s+g) bound.

With alpha=(s-g)/(1+g), alpha<1/6 supplies the previously required
Fourier-length margin. At Y=c0 X^alpha the powers of X balance at beta;
choosing a fixed sufficiently small c0 controls the Bonferroni term.
The dyadic rounding and eventual constants are harmless. Positive finite
witnesses give positive-dimensional intersections by spaced copies,
which justifies M_H>=M_C. No causal-container or ESS input is imported
into this proof during the merger.

5. ALL-PARAMETER EXCEPTIONAL FAMILY

The delayed recurrence follows the exact finite integer identity. The
partial-sum inequalities give the carry interval [-11,10]. The binary
register stores digit history; it is not the ternary value of that
history. Removing common powers of 3 normalizes a_0=b_0=1. A nonzero
finite witness would yield a path from the start and, at its first
relevant return, a predecessor (0,-1). Backward closure containing that
bad predecessor but excluding (0,0) therefore rules out the witness in
the correct direction. The reverse formula enumerates all four binary
choices and retains exactly the carries in the interval.

For k>=1 the invariant uses the 13 register types and two boundary
variants with the stated carries, with T_j=(16^j-1)/5 and the stated V.
The 16 displayed table rows encode 18 symbolic variants. I checked the
carry-set memberships, restricted destination annotations, additions in
P_+,U_+,W_+, and empty index ranges at k=1. The register high-bit bounds
are explicit for X=16^k>=16; the row registers are distinct. The three
identities T_(j+1)=16 T_j+3 and the two wrap identities yield the
destinations for every k. The invariant contains 38k+50 states,
contains (0,-1), excludes (0,0), and is closed under every legal reverse
transition. The separate k=0 table has 65 states and exhausts its 260
binary predecessor choices.

Thus the all-k assertion comes from a symbolic invariant and exact
identities for a parameter, not from testing a long finite list of k.
The supplied all-parameter symbolic checker complements the written
proof. Redundant numerical instances are correctly identified as
redundant instances. The elementary-obstruction avoidance uses the
modulo-3 property and the stated strict real interval; it does not
silently strengthen nonrepresentation to a 3-adic dimension assertion.
